Data Sources & Methodology

All data sources and calculation methods used in the Global Nuclear Database.

Source Code Quick Reference

[PRIS]IAEA Power Reactor Information System — primary source for reactor specifications, status, and generation data
[WNA]World Nuclear Association country and technology profiles — supplementary reactor data, planned projects
[EI-SR]Energy Institute Statistical Review of World Energy — global electricity generation totals
[WP]Wikipedia — cross-reference for reactor histories, shutdown dates, ownership
[NRC]U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission — U.S. reactor licensing and status
[CNEA]China Nuclear Energy Association — Chinese reactor fleet data

Primary Sources

PRIS IAEA Power Reactor Information System
https://pris.iaea.org
The PRIS database, maintained by the International Atomic Energy Agency, is the authoritative global reference for nuclear power reactor data. It is the primary source for this database.
Covers: Reactor name, type, model, capacity (gross/net MW), status, construction start, grid connection, commercial operation date, permanent shutdown date, geographic coordinates, country, owner, operator, annual electricity generation (GWh), reference unit power (MWe net).
WNA World Nuclear Association
https://world-nuclear.org/information-library
Country profiles and technology pages from WNA provide supplementary context on reactor fleet status, planned and proposed reactors, and national nuclear energy policies.
Covers: Planned and proposed reactors, under-construction project details, country nuclear energy profiles, vendor/export project information. Key page: Plans for New Reactors Worldwide.
EI-SR Energy Institute Statistical Review of World Energy
https://www.energyinst.org/statistical-review
Formerly the BP Statistical Review of World Energy, now published by the Energy Institute. Used for global total electricity generation figures (TWh) to compute nuclear's share of global electricity.
Covers: Global electricity generation by year (1970–2024), used in the "Nuclear Share of Global Electricity" chart.

Secondary & Cross-Reference Sources

WP Wikipedia
https://en.wikipedia.org
Wikipedia articles on individual reactors, nuclear power plants, and country nuclear programs are used as cross-references to verify data from primary sources. Particularly useful for historical shutdown dates, ownership changes, and reactor construction timelines.
Covers: Reactor histories, shutdown dates, ownership transfers, construction milestones, country nuclear fleet summaries (e.g., "Nuclear power in France").
NRC U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission
https://www.nrc.gov/reactors
Official U.S. nuclear regulatory body. Used to verify status, ownership, and licensing details for all U.S. commercial reactors.
Covers: U.S. reactor operating status, license holders, decommissioning status.
CNEA China Nuclear Energy Association
https://en.china-nea.cn
Provides data on China's rapidly expanding nuclear fleet, including new construction starts and grid connections that may not yet be reflected in IAEA PRIS.
Covers: Chinese reactor construction starts, grid connections, fleet status updates.

Methodology Notes

Generation Data Coverage Adjustment

Annual electricity generation data comes from IAEA PRIS [PRIS]. However, not all operational reactors report generation data for every year. For recent years (particularly 2022–2025), only a subset of the global fleet reports.

To estimate total global nuclear generation, we apply a coverage adjustment:

Adjusted TWh = Raw TWh x (Operational Reactors / Reporting Reactors)

This assumes non-reporting reactors generate at the same average rate as reporting ones. The dashboard displays the coverage percentage and raw vs. adjusted figures for transparency.

Nuclear Share of Global Electricity

The annual share chart divides coverage-adjusted nuclear generation [PRIS] by total global electricity generation [EI-SR] for each year from 1970 to 2024.

Global electricity totals are from the Energy Institute Statistical Review of World Energy (formerly BP Statistical Review). Nuclear generation uses PRIS data with coverage adjustment as described above.

Capacity Factor Calculation

Capacity factors are computed at query time from PRIS generation data:

CF = Electricity (GWh) / (Effective Gross Capacity (MW) / 1000 x 8760) x 100%

For reactors with documented capacity changes (uprates or derates), the effective gross capacity for each year is the most recent capacity in effect at year-end. For all other reactors, the current gross capacity is used. This assumes a full calendar year (8,760 hours). First-year and final-year capacity factors may be low due to partial-year operation.

Reactor Status Definitions

Reactor statuses follow IAEA PRIS conventions:

Operational — Connected to grid and available for electricity generation.

Under Construction — First concrete pour for the reactor building base mat has occurred [PRIS]. Site preparation work alone does not qualify.

Suspended — Reactor has been shut down for an extended period but has not been permanently closed. May be undergoing regulatory review for restart (e.g., post-Fukushima Japanese reactors) or in suspended construction/operation [PRIS].

Shutdown — Reactor has been permanently closed with no plans for restart. Includes reactors in active decommissioning.

Planned — Announced or approved but first concrete has not been poured. Includes projects with site licenses, vendor selections, or government commitments [WNA] [PRIS].

Reactor Age

Reactor age is computed from the commercial operation date recorded in PRIS, not the grid connection date or construction start date. Ages are computed dynamically at query time.

Plant Grouping on Maps

Multi-unit sites are grouped into a single map marker by matching coordinates rounded to 4 decimal places (~11m precision). The marker color reflects the "best" status at the plant: green if any reactor is operational, yellow for under construction only, etc. Clicking a marker shows all reactors at the plant regardless of filter.

Data Currency

Reactor specifications and status: Verified against IAEA PRIS, WNA, and Wikipedia as of March 2026.

Generation data: IAEA PRIS annual electricity generation, 1954–2025.

Global electricity totals: Energy Institute Statistical Review, 1970–2024.

Planned reactors: WNA, IAEA PRIS, and news sources as of March 2026.

Under construction: 66 reactors verified against IAEA PRIS (March 2026).
